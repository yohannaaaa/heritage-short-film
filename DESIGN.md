# DESIGN — The Heritage Short

## 1. Story Title

<!-- cspell: disable-next-line -->
**በአንድ ጠረጴዛ፣ በተለያዩ ዓለማት**

**English title:** At One Table, In Different Worlds

## 2. Story Idea

This film is a nostalgic reflection on how family life changes as people grow older and take on different responsibilities.

It explores the difference between the simple, spontaneous moments families once shared and the reality of modern family life, where people can be physically together but mentally occupied with completely different things.

The story is told through four scenes.

### Scene 1 — The Little Moments We Remember

The film opens with a familiar and humorous memory from family life.

My mother and uncle call my sister and me while they are at the gym. They tell us that they are coming home soon and ask us to clean the house and take some meat out of the refrigerator.

We confidently say yes, even though we are watching movies and have no intention of getting up immediately.

About fifteen or twenty minutes later, they call again to say that they are close to home. They ask whether we have finished everything.

That is when we realize we have completely forgotten the tasks. We rush around the house, trying to finish everything before they arrive.

Eventually, we manage to get everything done. My mother and uncle return from the gym, surprisingly energetic, while my sister and I are already exhausted from our last-minute cleaning.

They quickly prepare a meal, and we gather around a large shared plate called a <!-- cspell: disable-next-line -->**ትሪ (tiri)**.

We eat together, share food, laugh, and talk.

One of the recurring family jokes is about Wednesdays and Fridays, when we do not eat meat. My uncle dislikes these days and often complains about having to eat without meat. However, he loves my mother's cooking so much that we tease him:
<!-- cspell: disable-next-line -->
**"ጥረግ አርገህ ትበላለህ!"**

The moment captures something ordinary but meaningful: family jokes, shared food, familiar voices, and the comfort of being together.

The scene should feel warm, lively, slightly chaotic, and genuinely funny. It should show that happiness often comes from the simplest everyday experiences.

### Scene 2 — Time Passes

The atmosphere gradually changes.

The children grow older. Life moves forward. People get married, move out, establish their own homes, build careers, and develop families and responsibilities of their own.

The house that once felt full of constant activity becomes quieter.

This passage of time will be communicated visually rather than through lengthy explanations.

Possible visual transitions include:

- A family photograph showing everyone together.
- A suitcase being packed before someone moves out.
- An empty chair at the familiar dining table.
- A wedding photograph or a new family home.
- A calendar or changing family photographs showing the passage of years.

The purpose is to show that nobody necessarily chose to become distant. Life simply changed, and everyone followed their own path.

### Scene 3 — Together Again, But Differently

The film returns to the family dinner table in the present.

The family is physically together, perhaps during a visit or family gathering. The familiar food and the shared table are still there.

However, the atmosphere is different.

Everyone is occupied with their own responsibilities and digital world.

- One person is answering work emails.
- Someone is attending a virtual meeting.
- Someone is scrolling through social media.
- Someone is texting their spouse or children.
- Someone is watching a video with headphones.
- Another person is eating quietly while looking at their phone.

Even though everyone is sitting around the same table, nobody seems fully present.

The camera slowly moves around the table, showing each person individually and emphasizing how disconnected their attention has become.

The contrast with the first dinner should be clear. The same family, the same shared meal, and perhaps even the same jokes, but a very different atmosphere.

### Scene 4 — The Emotional Realization

One person eventually looks up and observes everyone around the table.

For a brief moment, the present-day scene is interrupted by sounds from the past: laughter, conversations, the sound of people sharing food, and familiar family jokes.

The audience is reminded of the earlier dinner, when everyone was fully engaged with one another.

The sounds gradually disappear, returning the audience to the quiet present.

The film ends with a reflective question:

**Are we really spending time together, or are we simply sharing the same space?**

The ending should not blame technology or suggest that modern responsibilities are wrong. Instead, it should remind viewers that family connection requires more than physical presence.

## 3. Cultural or Historical Significance

The film explores the emotional and cultural importance of family gatherings and shared meals in Ethiopian households.

It highlights familiar everyday experiences, including:

- Family members gathering around a shared <!-- cspell: disable-next-line -->**ትሪ (tiri)**.
- Eating together and sharing food.
- The preparation and enjoyment of home-cooked meals.
- Family members joking, teasing, and complaining with one another.
- The significance of familiar family routines and traditions.
- The role of shared meals in creating memories across generations.

The Wednesday and Friday fasting-day references add an authentic detail to the family's everyday life. The uncle's humorous complaints and the family's teasing demonstrate how even ordinary routines can become part of a family's shared identity.

Rather than documenting a particular historical event, the film preserves the emotional value of everyday family experiences that may gradually change as people grow older.

It also explores how modernization, technology, work, marriage, moving away, and changing responsibilities influence family relationships.

## 4. Intended Audience

The primary audience is Ethiopian families and communities.

The film is particularly intended for:

- Young Ethiopians who may recognize the transition from childhood family life to adult responsibilities.
- Parents and older family members who remember when family gatherings were more frequent and spontaneous.
- Ethiopian families living apart who may relate to the changing nature of family relationships.
- People who value family traditions, shared meals, and inter generational connections.

The story should be relatable even to viewers who have not experienced exactly the same family routines.

## 5. Audience Takeaway

The audience should reflect on how family relationships change as people grow older and life becomes more demanding.

The film should communicate that the most meaningful family memories are not necessarily created during special occasions. They often come from ordinary moments: eating together, joking, helping around the house, sharing food, and spending time with one another.

It should remind viewers that being physically present is not always the same as being emotionally present.

The central takeaway is that life moves forward, people grow up, and responsibilities increase, but the small moments of connection we once took for granted are worth appreciating.

The intention is not to criticize technology, marriage, careers, or modern life. It is to encourage people to make room for meaningful connections despite their busy lives.

## 6. Language

- Original narration: Amharic.
- Subtitles: English.
- Tone: Nostalgic, intimate, warm, humorous, and reflective.
- Dialogue: Natural Amharic family expressions and conversations.
- Narration: Emotionally expressive, with a storytelling approach rather than a formal documentary tone.

The opening should feel conversational and familiar. The narration should gradually become more reflective as the story moves from the past into the present.

## 7. Visual Style

**Cinematic and realistic.**

The film will use realistic Ethiopian household environments, familiar food, clothing, and everyday family interactions.

### Visual treatment of the past

The first scene should feel warm, lively, and intimate.

Visual characteristics:

- Warm golden and amber lighting.
- Natural household lighting and cozy interiors.
- Close-up shots of hands preparing food and setting the table.
- Details of the refrigerator, cleaning activities, and family members rushing around.
- Genuine expressions, laughter, and playful interactions.
- A large shared ትሪ placed at the center of the dining scene.
- Natural camera movement that makes the audience feel like they are part of the family.

The atmosphere should feel comfortable and lived-in rather than staged or luxurious.

### Visual treatment of the passage of time

The second scene should use gradual transitions and visual storytelling.

Visual characteristics:

- Family photographs.
- Moving boxes and suitcases.
- Changing household arrangements.
- Empty chairs.
- Different homes and family environments.

The transitions should communicate the passage of time without requiring extensive narration.

### Visual treatment of the present

The third scene should contrast with the opening.

Visual characteristics:

- Cooler or more neutral lighting.
- More individual framing rather than group interactions.
- Close-ups of phone screens and laptop meetings.
- People physically close to one another but visually separated by their devices.
- Less movement and fewer spontaneous interactions.
- Longer, quieter shots.

The present should still look realistic and comfortable. It should not be portrayed as a completely unhappy or miserable environment.

### Final visual treatment

The final scene should use a slow, emotional camera movement and subtle sound design.

The past and present may briefly overlap through sound, creating a contrast between remembered laughter and present-day silence.

## 8. Creative Intent

I chose this story because it reflects something personal and familiar about family life.

I wanted to capture the ordinary moments that once made being at home feel so special.

I remember moments when my mother and uncle would call my sister and me while they were at the gym, asking us to clean the house and prepare things before they arrived. We would say yes, continue watching movies, completely forget our responsibilities, and then rush around the house when they called to say they were close.

Even after all that rushing, they would return from the gym with more energy than we had.

We would eventually sit together around a large shared plate, eat my mother's cooking, laugh, and tease my uncle about Wednesdays and Fridays when there was no meat.

These moments were ordinary, but they created memories that stayed with us.

As time passes, family members grow older, get married, move away, and develop their own responsibilities. Even when everyone comes together again, the experience can be very different.

People may be sitting at the same table, but their attention is somewhere else.

I want to use this film to preserve the feeling of those everyday family moments and contrast them with the way family gatherings can look today.

My intention is to make viewers smile at the beginning, recognize themselves in the story, and feel a sense of nostalgia and reflection by the end.

Ultimately, this is a film about family, time, memory, and the importance of being present with the people we love.

## 8.1 Narrative Perspective and Ending

### Narrative Perspective

The story is experienced through the perspective of a child who has grown up and is remembering the family dinners and everyday experiences of their childhood.

The narrator is an adult reflecting on ordinary family moments that once felt normal but have become meaningful memories over time.

The film uses a personal, first-person storytelling approach. The audience experiences the memories through the younger version of the narrator.

### Ending

The film will have a quiet and emotional ending that leaves the audience thinking.

Rather than directly telling viewers what they should do, the final scene will allow them to reflect on the difference between being physically together and being emotionally present.

The final moment will briefly bring back the sounds of the family's earlier laughter before returning to the quiet present-day dinner.

### Character Representation

All characters will be fictionalized representations inspired by personal family memories.

They will not be intended as exact visual replicas of real family members.
